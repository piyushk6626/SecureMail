AI-assisted passive forensics for email crypto posture

SecureMail assesses SMTP, IMAP, and POP3 traffic in PCAP/PCAPNG files. It reconstructs sessions, checks STARTTLS/TLS/certificates against versioned rules, then uses ML only to prioritize unusual behavior.

Core rule: the design is deterministic first and AI assisted. Versioned rules decide whether an RFC or cryptographic requirement was violated. ML may flag unusual behavior and help prioritize work. It must never make the compliance call.
Zeek + TSharkPrimary analyzer + targeted decoder. Do not write another packet parser.
FastAPI stackPostgreSQL, RabbitMQ/Celery, content-addressed evidence, React reports.
Evidence statesMissing, encrypted, or incomplete data is never rendered as “secure.”
Defer complexityOpenSearch, K8s, advanced ML, and LLM wait until measured need.

1. Purpose

This document maps every requested deliverable to established solutions, recommends a stack and architecture, defines engineering/forensic/ML practices, and proposes a phased roadmap.

Every OBJECTIVE.MD deliverable to a proven tool plus SecureMail’s own layer.

2. Scope and non-goals

In scope

  • Offline PCAP / PCAPNG ingestion
  • Passive SMTP, IMAP, POP3 on standard and nonstandard ports
  • Best-effort bidirectional TCP reconstruction
  • SMTP STARTTLS, IMAP STARTTLS, POP3 STLS
  • Observable TLS handshakes and X.509 chains
  • Versioned crypto and email-policy checks
  • Evidence-linked findings, scoring, anomalies, dashboards, JSON/HTML/PDF
  • Single-node first, with a path to multi-node

Non-goals

  • Breaking encryption or recovering plaintext without authorized keys
  • Replacing Wireshark-class packet tools
  • Treating missing evidence as proof a session was secure
  • Active scanning of mail servers in the default workflow
  • Automatically changing server configuration
  • Letting ML/LLM make final compliance, attribution, legal, or IR decisions

2.3 Critical feasibility constraints

The objective says “complete” and “validate.” Passive evidence cannot guarantee those outcomes in every capture. SecureMail must say what it saw, not invent certainty.

1. Capture quality

Missing packets, asymmetric routing, truncation, midstream capture, drops, conflicting retransmissions, or malformed fragments can make a stream incomplete or ambiguous.

2. TLS 1.3 hides the rest

Messages after ServerHello, including the server certificate, are encrypted. Without authorized session secrets, cert extraction is normally unavailable.

3. ECH reduces identity

Encrypted ClientHello can hide SNI and other ClientHello metadata used for endpoint identity and correlation.

4. Historical validation needs history

“Valid at capture time” needs capture timestamp, identity, trust-store snapshot, intermediates, and time-appropriate revocation. Relays may also need historical MX, DNSSEC/TLSA, or MTA-STS.

5. Opportunistic SMTP is context dependent

Cleartext fallback on port 25 is not by itself proof of non-compliance unless DANE, MTA-STS, REQUIRETLS, or an organizational policy required authenticated TLS.

Evidence states — never collapse these into “secure”

“No weakness detected” must never be rendered as “secure” when relevant checks are incomplete, not observable, or indeterminate.

observed
Directly decoded from captured bytes
verified
Independently checked against a named policy or trust source
inferred
Derived from observable protocol facts
incomplete
Affected by capture loss or truncation
conflicting
Multiple valid reconstructions or sources disagree
not_observable
Encrypted or absent by protocol design
indeterminate
Required context is unavailable

3.2 Alternatives and commercial benchmarks

NetworkMiner

Useful for analyst cross-check and reconstructed mail artifacts. Not a complete cryptographic posture engine. Some automation is edition-dependent.

Xplico

Shows SMTP/IMAP/POP3 reconstruction. Age and narrower maintenance make it a validation reference, not a primary dependency.

Security Onion / Malcolm

Mature SOC integration around Zeek, Suricata, retention, and dashboards. Neither supplies the email-specific policy, historical PKI, confidence, and report model.

Corelight

Closest commercial benchmark for operationalized Zeek telemetry and air-gapped network security monitoring.

NDR vendors

ExtraHop, NetWitness, Darktrace, Vectra, Stamus: useful quality benchmarks. Proprietary models conflict with open-source-first. None verified to cover the full deliverable set.

EndaceProbe

Relevant for lossless high-rate capture retention. It is a capture appliance, not an email cryptographic posture analyzer.

Commercial products are product-quality benchmarks. Do not assume they provide protocol coverage or forensic guarantees without a proof of concept.

5. Recommended architecture

Follow the lanes top to bottom. The API never runs packet analysis. Workers talk to analyzers. PostgreSQL is the source of truth. ML cannot rewrite findings.

5.2 Processing stages

Evidence intake

Validate file signatures and structure, not only extension or MIME. SHA-256 before analysis. Immutable content-addressed original. Record case, custodian, source, capture point, timezone, interface, snap length, filters, drops, clock source, transfer history. Read-only working copy plus conversion manifest if PCAPNG must be normalized.

Capture preflight

Inspect packet counts, interfaces, timestamp resolution, truncation, malformed blocks, duplicates, protocol distribution. Reject resource-policy violations early.

Primary analysis

Pinned Zeek image with no network. Produce connection, protocol, TLS, X.509, file, and notice logs. Keep raw analyzer output as an immutable artifact.

Targeted corroboration

TShark with a fixed argument array and bounded fields. Only sessions that need detailed frame evidence. Avoid unbounded full-tree JSON on large captures.

Normalization

Analyzer records → versioned Pydantic schemas. Every fact links to capture hash, flow key, direction, frames, byte offsets, analyzer version, and confidence.

Deterministic evaluation

Named, effective-dated email / TLS / X.509 / org profiles. Produce finding codes, severity, evidence confidence, rationale, standards refs, remediation template IDs.

Advisory analytics

Endpoint/time-window features. Separate novelty/anomaly score and reason codes. Never suppress or rewrite deterministic findings.

Reporting and visualization

Freeze canonical JSON. Render self-contained HTML and PDF. Dashboards keep observed facts, deterministic conclusions, ML output, and analyst decisions in separate lanes.

5.3 Clean application boundaries

Dependency inversion and a single composition root. CPU-heavy or subprocess analysis never runs in a FastAPI request process.

API routers — validate HTTP, invoke one use case, map typed results to responses
Application operations — orchestrate workflows; do not import DB, Celery, Zeek, or TShark
Domain rules and schemas — pure where possible; Pydantic models or frozen dataclasses
Protocol ports — small interfaces for persistence and analyzers
Adapters — PostgreSQL, artifacts, queue, Zeek/TShark, reports, ML

Suggested layout

src/securemail/ api/ # FastAPI routers, dependencies, response schemas application/ # RORO use cases and workflow orchestration domain/ evidence/ # Canonical packet, flow, handshake, certificate models policies/ # Pure, effective-dated rule evaluators findings/ # Finding, score, recommendation models ports/ # Protocol interfaces for persistence and analyzers adapters/ analyzers/ # Zeek and TShark subprocess adapters persistence/ # SQLAlchemy repositories artifacts/ # Content-addressed evidence storage queue/ # Celery/RabbitMQ integration reports/ # JSON, Jinja2, WeasyPrint ml/ # Feature and model adapters workers/ # Idempotent task entry points bootstrap.py # Composition root tests/ unit/ integration/ golden_pcaps/ end_to_end/

Avoid a generic utils package. Put code in the domain or adapter that owns the responsibility.

5.5 Idempotency and consistency

Deterministic run identity

(capture_sha256, analyzer_bundle_digest, normalization_schema_version, policy_pack_version, trust_store_digest, configuration_digest)

Queue payloads

IDs and artifact hashes only. Workers may run more than once; writes are upserts. Authoritative job state lives in PostgreSQL, not Celery results.

Outbox + cancellation

Transactional outbox so DB state and task publication cannot diverge. Cancellation is cooperative and leaves a complete audit record.

Partial failure

Produces a bounded, inspectable run state. It never silently drops a stage.

5.6 Scaling model

Scale across captures first. Each capture is an independent unit and can go to a worker pool selected by file size and analyzer needs.

Do not split one capture by byte offset or arbitrary time range — TCP sessions cross boundaries. If single-capture parallelism becomes necessary:

  1. Preclassify packets using a symmetric bidirectional flow key.
  2. Keep every TCP flow on one worker.
  3. Preserve original frame numbers and timestamps.
  4. Merge only canonical flow/session records, not partially reassembled streams.

Use PostgreSQL partitioning, staging tables, and COPY for high-volume session records. Add OpenSearch only after indexed PostgreSQL is shown insufficient. Add ClickHouse only for measured, very-high-volume analytical aggregation.

6.2 License and supply-chain controls

  • Keep Zeek’s permissive core as the primary integration boundary.
  • Treat TShark, Suricata, and Scapy as separately invoked GPL tools. Preserve notices and get legal review before a bundled appliance.
  • RabbitMQ is MPL-2.0. PostgreSQL, React, Vite, ECharts, cryptography, scikit-learn, and WeasyPrint are generally permissive.
  • Review Grafana, Loki, and Tempo licensing for the chosen redistribution model.
  • JA4 family components do not all share one license. Review each fingerprint component.
  • ML runtime code and model weights have separate licenses; both must be approved and inventoried.
  • Air-gap update bundle: signed OCI images, wheels, OS packages, npm artifacts, trust stores, policy packs, vuln data, models.
  • Pin images by digest, generate SBOMs, verify signatures, and test rollback.

7. Standards baseline

Policy rules must be data, not hard-coded parser behavior. Every rule includes a stable ID, source, applicability, effective date, severity, rationale, and test cases.

7.1 Email protocols and transport security

Core RFCs

  • RFC 5321 SMTP
  • RFC 3207 SMTP STARTTLS
  • RFC 6409 message submission
  • RFC 8314 / RFC 8997 TLS for submission and access; implicit TLS preferred for client access
  • RFC 7817 historical email server identity
  • RFC 9051 IMAP4rev2
  • RFC 1939 / RFC 2449 POP3 and CAPA
  • RFC 2595 TLS for IMAP/POP3, including STLS

Relay policy

  • RFC 7672 SMTP DANE
  • RFC 8461 MTA-STS
  • RFC 8460 SMTP TLS Reporting
  • RFC 8689 REQUIRETLS
  • IANA ports are hints, not protocol proof

Separate profiles

  1. SMTP relay on 25
  2. Submission on 465/587
  3. IMAP on 143/993
  4. POP3 on 110/995
  5. Org-specific internal relays

A client-access rule must not be applied blindly to opportunistic SMTP relay.

7.2 TLS and cryptography

  • BCP 195 current IETF TLS/DTLS recommendations
  • RFC 8996 deprecates TLS 1.0/1.1
  • RFC 9325 secure use of TLS/DTLS
  • RFC 9846 current TLS 1.3 spec
  • RFC 9852 TLS 1.3 for newly designed protocols — does not automatically make TLS 1.2 non-conformant for existing email
  • RFC 10015 TLS 1.2 key-exchange; no static RSA or finite-field DH/DHE
  • RFC 9155 deprecates MD5 and SHA-1 in TLS signatures
  • RFC 9849 Encrypted ClientHello
  • RFC 10024 hybrid ML-KEM/(EC)DHE groups
  • IANA TLS Parameters; NIST SP 800-52r2, 800-131A, FIPS 186-5, SP 800-57

Required profiles

ietf_current · nist_federal · historical_at_capture · organization_<name>

Prohibited / failed under current general policy

  • SSL 2.0, SSL 3.0, TLS 1.0, TLS 1.1
  • NULL, export, RC4, or < 112-bit effective security
  • TLS 1.2 static RSA and finite-field DH/DHE (RFC 10015)
  • MD5 or SHA-1 TLS digital signatures
  • RSA cert keys below 2048 bits under current IETF guidance

“Not FIPS-approved” and “cryptographically weak” are different findings. NIST SP 800-52 Rev. 2 is a selectable profile, not a universal definition of security.

7.3 X.509 and identity

RFC 5280 path validation. RFC 9525 current service identity (SAN matching; Common Name fallback removed). RFC 7817 may still be a separately labeled historical email compatibility result.

SNI is evidence, not always the intended MX/service identity. A certificate cannot be identity-validated merely against names contained in that same certificate. Report both the policy applied and the reference identity source.

7.4 TCP, IP, and capture formats

RFC 9293 TCP. RFC 8200 IPv6 including fragments. PCAP and PCAPNG remain IETF drafts at the research cutoff.

Prefer PCAPNG at intake when interface and timestamp metadata is valuable, but preserve it unchanged. A derived PCAP may lose interface, name-resolution, comment, packet-drop, or decryption-secret metadata and therefore needs a conversion manifest.

8. Standard engineering and forensic practices

8.1 Evidence integrity and chain of custody

Follow NIST SP 800-86, NIST IR 8387, and the scope of ISO/IEC 27037 / 27041 / 27042 / 27043.

  • Preserve the original file byte-for-byte; analyze verified working copies.
  • SHA-256 at intake; verify fixity after transfer, restore, and export.
  • Record authorization, case ID, source/custodian, acquiring person, timestamp/timezone, capture point, interface, filters, snap length, drops, clock source/offset, and each custody event.
  • Append-only, access-controlled custody records stored separately from the evidence file.
  • Detect captured_len < original_len, malformed blocks, gaps, conflicting retransmissions, reassembly ambiguity.
  • Preserve competing interpretations when evidence does not support one deterministic TCP reconstruction.
  • Never “repair” the evidential original.
  • Export a manifest of every artifact hash and tool/version digest.

8.2 Hostile-input processing

Treat every capture, decoded banner, certificate string, and report field as hostile.

Sandbox

Non-root, network-disabled analyzers. Read-only evidence mounts and root FS. Dropped capabilities, seccomp, PID/CPU/memory quotas, timeouts, bounded output.

Limits

Reject impossible block lengths, excessive nesting, decompression bombs, and configured packet/stream/chain limits before large allocation.

Uploads

Stream large uploads to quarantine while hashing. Do not load them into API memory.

Commands

Never build analyzer commands through a shell. Fixed executable + argument list with allowlisted options.

Output

Escape all decoded content in dashboard, HTML, and PDF. Keep report rendering isolated from packet analysis and the API.

No outbound

Disable DNS, HTTP, AIA, OCSP, CRL, MTA-STS, model downloads, and telemetry in analysis workers.

Baselines: OWASP File Upload Cheat Sheet, ASVS 5.0, API Security Top 10:2023, NIST SSDF SP 800-218, NIST SP 800-228.

8.3 Authentication, authorization, and auditing

  • OIDC + MFA per organizational policy.
  • Authorize every case, capture, run, finding, report, and export. UI visibility is not authorization.
  • Separate analyst, reviewer, case manager, evidence custodian, and platform administrator roles where required.
  • Step-up approval for export, deletion, retention override, model/rule promotion, and trust-store changes.
  • Encrypt captures, session secrets, extracted credentials, reports, and backups at rest.
  • Append-only audit events. Do not put payloads, credentials, private/session keys, or unnecessary personal data in logs.
  • Rate-limit uploads, searches, exports, and expensive analysis.

8.4 Privacy and retention

Captured traffic may contain credentials, mail content, addresses, identifiers, and organizational secrets. Offline deployment does not remove privacy duties. There is no universally correct retention period.

  • Documented authority, purpose, case scope, and data owner before ingestion.
  • Minimize stored payloads when cryptographic metadata is sufficient.
  • Segregate cases; review access periodically; retention by evidence class and jurisdiction.
  • Legal holds that suspend deletion.
  • Verify deletion of working copies, caches, exports, backups, session keys, extracted payloads, and ML features after expiry.
  • Do not reuse case evidence for ML training without separate authorization, minimization, and governance.
  • Support a DPIA where monitoring creates high privacy risk. GDPR Arts. 5, 25, 32, 35; NIST Privacy Framework; ISO/IEC 27701.

8.5 Reproducibility and report integrity

Every report must include original and working-copy hashes; case/capture/run IDs; capture quality and visibility limits; evidence/frame refs; analyzer/OS/container/dependency/normalizer/rule-pack/trust-store/model versions; configuration, timezone, seed, thresholds; applied standards profile and effective date; external evidence hashes; observed facts vs deterministic conclusions vs ML vs analyst conclusions as separate sections; stage errors, suppressed findings, exceptions, and indeterminate checks.

Use RFC 8785 canonical JSON before hashing or signing. RFC 3339/RFC 9557 timestamps. Optional W3C PROV. RFC 3161 trusted timestamps for proof of existence. PDF/A is archival presentation; canonical JSON remains authoritative.

8.6 Testing and validation

Deterministic / parser

  • Unit-test every rule, transition, and policy-version boundary
  • Golden captures for SMTP/IMAP/POP3, implicit TLS, STARTTLS success/fail, TLS 1.2/1.3, fragments, missing SYN/FIN, truncation, malformed, IPv4/IPv6, nonstandard ports
  • Differential Zeek vs TShark
  • Hypothesis + isolated fuzz of ASN.1, TLS, capture files, TCP reassembly
  • Exact frame/evidence refs, not only finding codes

Application

  • Operations against stubbed Protocol interfaces
  • Migrations, outbox, redelivery, idempotency, fixity, analyzer adapters
  • E2E: upload, cancel, review, drill-down, authz, export
  • Report snapshots with pinned fonts/renderers
  • Restore, legal-hold, retention, DR exercises

Performance

  • Ingest hashing throughput
  • Packets/bytes per second by protocol mix
  • Peak memory per GiB and large TCP stream
  • Queue wait and E2E time by capture-size class
  • PG insert/query/dashboard latency; report time/size; concurrent capacity; malformed/exhaustion behavior

Publish test hardware and configuration with all performance claims.

8.7 Observability and operations

  • Case-safe correlation ID through API, outbox, queue, worker, and report stages.
  • Structured JSON logs with secret/payload redaction.
  • OTel traces for stage duration and adapter calls.
  • Bounded-cardinality metrics: queue depth, stage latency, failures, retries, worker resources, capture sizes, findings, report jobs.
  • Alert on stalled queues, repeated parser crashes, fixity failures, storage thresholds, backup failures, policy/model signature failures.
  • Back up PostgreSQL and evidence manifests; immutable snapshots for artifacts; test restore in isolation and verify hashes.
  • Define RPO/RTO before introducing HA.

9. AI/ML design and governance

9.1 Authority boundary — keep these separate

1. Standards severity

Deterministic result from a named policy. This is authoritative.

2. Evidence confidence

Quality and completeness of the observation.

3. Anomaly level

Deviation from an endpoint or peer-group baseline. Advisory only.

An optional fourth output — predicted remediation priority — must be named after its actual target, not described as a probability that a system is “insecure.”

9.2 Feature design

Session

Protocol/role, upgrade state, offered/selected versions/ciphers/groups/signatures, AEAD and FS indicators, resumption/PSK/0-RTT, alerts, handshake duration, retransmissions, resets, fingerprints, visible cert properties, capture quality.

Endpoint window

TLS-version distribution, STARTTLS success rate, cleartext ratio, weak-config ratio, failure rate, cert/fingerprint churn, first-seen values, rare pairings, deviation from historical baseline.

Context

Asset role, criticality, exposure, environment, maintenance window, data sensitivity, accepted-risk expiry, capture point, visibility class.

Do not use raw IPs, names, serial numbers, or certificate identities as direct supervised features unless encoding and validation show the model is not memorizing one organization.

9.3 Model progression

  1. Start with median/MAD, quantiles, categorical rarity, and change-point rules.
  2. Evaluate Isolation Forest on endpoint-window features after enough baseline history.
  3. Compare any advanced model against these simple baselines.
  4. Train supervised operational-priority models only after analysts produce consistent, independently governed labels.
  5. Begin with regularized logistic regression; compare shallow GBTs only with measured benefit and stable explanations.
  6. Deploy every learned model in shadow mode before it can affect workflow.

Avoid autoencoders and sequence models in early releases. They need more data, are harder to explain, and can reproduce environment-specific noise.

9.4 Evaluation

Supervised

PR-AUC, per-tier precision/recall, critical recall, precision at the analyst’s daily review budget, Brier/calibration, cost-weighted errors, temporal and cross-organization holdouts. Split by time, endpoint/certificate family, and organization — random splits leak identity.

Anomaly

Alerts per 1,000 endpoint-days, precision among top-K reviewed, detection delay for seeded changes, stability during maintenance, new vs established endpoints.

Every anomaly must explain a concrete deviation

“First TLS 1.0 selection for this MX in 180 days.”

“Certificate issuer changed and handshake failures increased from 0.2% to 18%.”

“A new server fingerprint appeared in 4 of 2,800 sessions.”

9.5 False-positive and drift controls

  • Gate conclusions on capture quality and observability.
  • Deduplicate repeated sessions into endpoint-level findings.
  • Role/site peer groups and minimum-support thresholds.
  • Hysteresis and maintenance-window suppression.
  • Owner-approved exceptions with reason, reviewer, and expiry.
  • Quarantine analyst feedback until reviewed; never retrain automatically from clicks or suppression.
  • Monitor feature missingness, categorical novelty, score distribution, alert volume, visibility changes, calibration.
  • Treat sensor relocation or a new capture point as topology change before labeling it model drift.

9.6 Model and AI security

Version and hash the feature schema, dataset, label policy, training code, model, calibrator, thresholds, recommendation templates, prompts, and local model weights.

  • Malformed/fractured traffic and fingerprint mimicry
  • Poisoned imported baselines or analyst feedback
  • Model, trust-store, and policy-pack tampering
  • Malicious ASN.1/certificate parser inputs
  • Resource exhaustion
  • Forged PCAP timestamps
  • NAT/load-balancer aggregation
  • Indirect prompt injection from captured text
  • Evasion from TLS 1.3 and ECH visibility limits

Governance: NIST AI RMF 1.0, NIST AI 600-1, ISO/IEC 42001, ISO/IEC 23894.

9.7 Optional local LLM

May

  • Summarize verified findings for analyst or executive audience
  • Explain a curated remediation template
  • Group related findings
  • Answer questions over a bundled, versioned standards corpus with citations

Must not

  • Parse captures as the primary detector
  • Decide compliance or final severity
  • Execute generated commands
  • Access the network
  • Receive raw email bodies by default
  • Treat captured content as instructions

Use schema-constrained I/O, fixed token limits, citation validation, evidence IDs, an abstention path, and full model/prompt provenance.

10. Suggested enhancements

10.1 Highest priority

1. Capture observability score

Show what proportion of expected sessions, handshakes, and certificate checks were observable and why data was missing.

2. As-of-capture vs current policy

Separate “acceptable when captured” from “requires remediation today.”

3. Evidence bundle per finding

Minimal packet slices, normalized facts, raw analyzer records, policy version, and hashes for independent verification.

4. Cryptographic asset inventory / CBOM

Endpoints, versions, ciphers, groups, keys, certificates, issuers, fingerprints, owners, first/last seen.

5. Regression and blast-radius views

When posture changed, and how many sessions, clients, domains, and assets were affected.

6. Exception workflow

Owner, business reason, compensating controls, reviewer, expiry; automatically reopen expired exceptions.

10.2 Policy and ecosystem enrichment

  • Import signed/historical DNS, MX, DNSSEC/TLSA, MTA-STS, and TLS-RPT evidence.
  • Compare multiple sensor perspectives without duplicating findings.
  • Import authorized endpoint/session-secret telemetry for TLS 1.3 visibility.
  • Export stable finding codes and evidence links to SIEM/SOAR and case systems.
  • Configuration migration simulator for compatibility and expected posture change before rollout.
  • Non-sensitive lab capture generator and public golden corpus.

10.3 Cryptographic agility

  • Inventory Encrypted ClientHello visibility changes.
  • Recognize standardized hybrid ML-KEM/(EC)DHE groups.
  • Report PQC readiness and unknown algorithms without treating absence of PQC as a current email security failure.
  • Make algorithm/group registries and policy packs independently updateable and signed.

13. Decision summary

1
Zeek is the primary analyzer. TShark is a targeted corroborating decoder.
2
Build a canonical evidence and policy layer rather than another packet parser.
3
Start with FastAPI, Celery/RabbitMQ, PostgreSQL, content-addressed evidence, React, and deterministic reports.
4
Defer OpenSearch, Kubernetes, advanced ML, and an LLM until measured needs justify their operational and governance cost.
5
Capture quality, observability, provenance, and historical policy are first-class data.
6
Deterministic standards findings are authoritative. ML is explicitly advisory.
7
Report reproducibility, chain of custody, parser isolation, privacy, offline supply-chain updates, and licensing are product requirements from the first release.
